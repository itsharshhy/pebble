# Maintainer wellbeing
*Phase: Maintenance · A calm app shouldn't be built by a stressed person.*

Open-source maintainer burnout is widely reported: unpaid work, endless requests, and a feeling
of responsibility to strangers. These rules protect whoever builds Pebble the same way the app
protects its users.

## Rules
1. **One thing in "Doing".** Never more. ([Process model](../01-planning/process-model.md#planning-board-github-projects))
2. **"Done when" means done.** When the line at the bottom of a page is true, stop. Polishing past it is the overthinking talking.
3. **No guilt deadlines.** A missed week is just a paused loop.
4. **Write it down, then let it go.** A new idea at 1 a.m. goes in a GitHub issue labelled `idea`, and you deal with it at the monthly review. It's Pebble's own method ([S2](../00-foundation/science.md#s2)), applied to the project.
5. **You may say no.** To features, to contributors, to deadlines. Kindly, with a reason, and without apology spirals.
6. **Use your own app.** If building Pebble ever makes you more anxious than using it calms you, pause the project. Principle 1 applies to you too.

## When stuck
| Feeling | Do this |
|---|---|
| "This isn't perfect" | Check the "Done when" line. If it's met, ship it. |
| "Someone already built this" | Re-read [Vision](../00-foundation/vision.md). Success level 1 is *your* use. |
| "I don't understand this code" | Ask the AI helper to explain it line by line. Not knowing yet is the normal state of learning. |
| "Too many ideas" | Write them all as issues. Close the tab. Pick one loop. |
| "Nobody uses it" | Expected. Small and helpful beats big and hollow. |

## Done when
You can take a week off without the project, or you, falling apart.
