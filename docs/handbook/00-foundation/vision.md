# Vision
*Phase: Foundation · Read once, revisit when unsure what Pebble is for.*

## The problem
An overthinking mind makes forecasts all day: *"They're upset with me." "The presentation will go
badly." "I'll miss the deadline."* Each forecast feels urgent and true. But the mind almost never
**checks back**. When the feared thing doesn't happen, the worry just fades quietly and the brain
never updates. Next time, the same forecast feels just as believable.

Research on the **Worry Outcome Journal** suggests that collecting this missing evidence helps. In
one study of people with generalized anxiety disorder, 91.4% of tracked worries didn't come true,
and the people with more untrue worries saw larger symptom drops
(LaFreniere & Newman, 2020; see [Science S1](science.md#s1)).

## The solution
Pebble is a small, calm **desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux**. It runs entirely on your
own computer: no website, no account, no internet needed.
1. **Set a pebble down:** write a worry in one short line.
2. **Choose when to look back:** tomorrow, in 3 days, in a week, or on a date you pick.
3. **Check back:** on that day Pebble asks one question: *Did it happen?*
4. **See your garden:** a quiet, private record of how often your forecasts came true.

## The metaphor
Each worry is a **pebble you've been carrying**. Writing it down lets you set it down. Checking
back shows you it was lighter than it felt. "Let it go" drops it into a still pond. Over time you
see a garden of stones you no longer carry. The metaphor drives the visuals (round, soft, natural)
and the words ("set down", "let go", never "log" or "delete").

## Who it's for
- **Everyday overthinkers** without a diagnosis, who'd never call themselves "anxious" but replay and predict.
- **Privacy-conscious people** who won't put their inner life on someone's cloud, and want an app they can install, own and back up as a file.
- **People in therapy** whose therapist assigned a worry log (a standard CBT exercise).

## Who it's not for
- People in **crisis**. Pebble points them to real help ([Safety](../08-safety-ethics/safety.md)).
- People right after a **trauma**, when detailed writing can increase distress.
- People who want an **AI therapist** or a chat companion.

## What success looks like (honestly)
| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Real** | The maintainer uses Pebble for 30 days and finds it useful. *This alone justifies the project.* |
| **2. Shared** | 10 people outside the maintainer's circle use it for 2+ weeks. |
| **3. Trusted** | A therapist recommends it, or a stranger contributes code. |

Stars, downloads and virality are **not** goals. Mental-health apps lose most users within weeks
(see [Science S14](science.md#s14)). We'd rather be small and genuinely helpful than big and hollow.

## Non-goals (what Pebble will never become)
- A social network, or anything with sharing or likes.
- A mood tracker full of charts.
- A product that makes money from user data.
- A replacement for therapy.

## Done when
You can explain Pebble to a friend in two sentences and say who it's *not* for.
